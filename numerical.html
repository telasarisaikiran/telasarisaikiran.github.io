FSI Models for Cavitation and Gas Bubbles in Biological Plants

Status: Ongoing Research Project

Project Overview

This ongoing research project focuses on understanding cavitation, gas bubble dynamics and embolism formation in biological plant xylem using Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) modelling techniques. The study aims to investigate how air bubbles nucleate, grow and interact with fluid flow inside plant vascular systems under different environmental conditions.

Research Motivation

Plants transport water through microscopic xylem vessels. Under drought or freezing conditions, air bubbles may form inside these vessels causing cavitation and embolism. These phenomena can interrupt water transport and affect plant growth and survival.

Understanding bubble formation and transport mechanisms is essential for advancing research in plant hydraulics and improving our understanding of drought resistance in plants.

Current Objectives

  • Study cavitation phenomena in plant xylem vessels.
  • Investigate gas bubble growth and transport mechanisms.
  • Develop Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) models.
  • Analyze embolism formation under different conditions.
  • Understand the influence of vessel geometry on flow behaviour.

Methodology

The project involves literature review, numerical modelling and simulation-based analysis. Computational tools are being used to study fluid flow behaviour, pressure variations, bubble dynamics and interactions between fluid and surrounding biological structures.

Software & Tools

  • ANSYS Mechanical
  • CFD Post

  • Scientific Literature Analysis

Current Progress

  • Bubble dynamics and cavitation mechanisms under investigation.
  • Simulation setup and validation currently in progress.

Expected Outcomes

  • Numerical models for bubble growth and transport.
  • Insights into embolism formation and recovery processes.
  • Contribution towards plant hydraulics and biofluid mechanics research.
